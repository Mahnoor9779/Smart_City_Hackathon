Read this before you pick anything

Thirty-nine features are listed below. A five-person team should ship roughly twelve. The failure mode at every hackathon is a demo with fourteen half-working features and no story. Build the Spine completely, add two or three Decision features you can finish, and spend real time on Access. It is a named judging criterion that almost every team ignores, which makes it the cheapest points on the board.

Spine

What the brief literally asks for

Without these you have not answered Problem Statement 1. Finish all four before anyone starts on anything below.

S01EFFORT M
RELTEC

One map, live layers, toggled independently

A single MapLibre canvas carrying air quality, weather, OSM facilities and population density, each switchable. Open-Meteo's air-quality and forecast APIs are keyless and CORS-open, so two genuinely live sources are running within the first hour with no backend at all.

Why it scoresThe brief's one hard requirement: two or more live data sources into a single map. Everything else is optional; this is not.
S02EFFORT M
TECFEA

Union council boundaries as the unit of analysis

Every number on the page resolves to a named administrative area, not a floating dot. Pull boundaries from OSM via the HOT Export Tool, snap population and sensor readings onto them, and you have a base that every later feature reuses.

Why it scoresOfficials think in administrative units. A map that says "AQI 214 in Shalimar Town" is usable; a heat blob is not. This is also what makes the whole thing rankable, comparable and sortable later.
S03EFFORT S
RELUXCLR

Know Your Neighbourhood. Search, profile, printable summary

Type a neighbourhood, get one page: population, current air quality, nearest hospital and school with travel time, road connectivity, active alerts. Export it as a clean one-page PDF.

Why it scoresNamed explicitly in the problem statement's starter ideas. Cheap to build, and it is the best vehicle for your demo. A judge understands it in four seconds without explanation.
S04EFFORT S
FEACLRRWA

Live source panel with an empty seat for the official feeds

A visible panel listing every connected source, its last-updated timestamp and its health. Below the live ones, greyed out but wired: Suthra Punjab fleet, Punjab EPA monitors, PITB One Map. Each behind a real adapter interface with a mock implementation you can toggle on stage.

Why it scoresThe brief names the two-track constraint itself: build on open data, design so an official feed plugs in later without a rebuild. Most teams say this in a slide. Showing a mock adapter flip on is the version judges believe.
Decision

Turning display into decision

The brief's sharpest line: transform raw data into actionable information rather than simply displaying it. This band is where a dashboard stops being a dashboard. Pick two or three.

D01EFFORT S
INNIMPCLR

Exposure-weighted metrics. Multiply everything by people

Never show a bare reading. WorldPop × AQI gives people exposed, not "AQI 214". Distance to a hospital becomes "12,400 residents beyond a 15-minute drive from emergency care." Same data, completely different sentence.

Why it scoresHighest impact per hour on this entire list. It is a multiplication, it takes an afternoon, and it converts every panel from a measurement into a consequence. WorldPop's own framing invites it: population maps show who is affected by whatever you map.
D02EFFORT L
TECIMPINN

Road-network travel time, not straight-line distance

Run OSRM or Valhalla over a Lahore OSM extract and compute real isochrones, 5, 10, 15 minutes by road. A hospital 800m away across an unbridged canal is not 800m away.

Why it scoresEvery other team will draw circles. Circles are wrong in a city cut by canals, railways and the Ravi, and a technical judge will know it on sight. Clearest available signal of Technical Execution.
D03EFFORT M
IMPCLRRWA

Civic Access Score. One composite index, one ranked table

Fold access to health, education and green space, air quality and population density into a single transparent score per union council. Publish the weights and let the user change them. Then rank every UC worst to best.

Why it scoresA ranked list is a to-do list. It converts a map into "these five areas need attention first," which is the exact output a city official can act on. Keep the formula visible. A black-box index reads as hand-waving.
D04EFFORT L
INNIMPTECRWA

Siting optimiser. Where the next clinic should actually go

Greedy maximum coverage over the access surface: given current facilities and the population grid, compute the location that brings the most underserved people inside a 15-minute threshold. Output a ranked shortlist with the population each site newly covers.

Why it scoresThis is the feature that answers "think beyond traditional dashboards." It is prescriptive, not descriptive. The system makes a recommendation and defends it with a number. If you build exactly one differentiator, build this one.
D05EFFORT M
INNUXCLR

What-if mode. Drop a pin, watch coverage recompute

Let the user place a hypothetical hospital, school or park anywhere on the map and immediately see the access surface redraw and the affected population count update. Runs entirely in the browser over precomputed reachability sets, so there is no routing call and no perceptible delay.

Why it scoresThe most demo-able thing on this list. It is interactive, it is instant, and it hands the judge the mouse. Pairs with D04: the optimiser proposes, the user tests.
D06EFFORT S
UXCLR

Side-by-side comparison of two areas

Two union councils, same metrics, one screen. Difference bars rather than two separate readouts.

Why it scoresInequality between neighbourhoods is the story this data actually tells, and comparison is the only layout that tells it in one glance.
Predictive

The forward-looking layer

The theme asks for a view that is "live, predictive, and citizen-facing." Most teams will deliver live and citizen-facing and quietly skip predictive. Open-Meteo hands you forecast data for free, so there is no excuse.

P01EFFORT M
INNIMPRELRWA

Smog risk forecast, 48–72 hours out

Combine Open-Meteo's PM2.5 forecast with wind speed, wind direction and temperature inversion proxies to flag which union councils are heading into hazardous air, before it arrives. Show the arrival window, not just a number.

Why it scoresLahore's smog season is the city's signature public health emergency and it is genuinely predictable from free forecast data. Nothing else here lands harder with a Lahore audience. Your Relevance and Social Impact scores in one feature.
P02EFFORT L
INNIMPRWA

Monsoon urban flood risk

Rainfall forecast crossed with terrain low points from a Copernicus DEM and built-up density from OSM, to flag likely ponding areas hours ahead. Weight by population to rank which flood spots matter most.

Why it scoresThis is the Rio parallel the brief opens with, done for Lahore: rainfall crosses a threshold, warnings reach at-risk neighbourhoods. Drainage-poor low-lying areas flood every monsoon and the pattern is known locally, which makes your output checkable against lived experience.
P03EFFORT M
INNIMP

Urban heat island exposure

Copernicus land surface temperature against population and tree cover, precomputed as static tiles at build time. Ranks neighbourhoods by heat exposure per resident.

Why it scoresAdds satellite data credibly without a live Earth Engine pipeline, and heat mortality is the under-discussed twin of Lahore's air quality problem.
P04EFFORT M
IMPRWAUX

Threshold alerts that actually leave the building

Subscribe to an area; when air quality, rainfall or heat crosses a threshold, an alert goes out. Web push for smartphones and email for officials both ship. SMS for feature phones is scoped out: it needs a paid gateway, and naming it as roadmap is more honest than gesturing at it.

Why it scoresThe brief's opening example is Rio warning at-risk neighbourhoods within seconds. A dashboard nobody opens saves nobody. The alert is what makes the system operational rather than observational. The SMS path is what makes it real in Pakistan.
P05EFFORT S
TECCLR

Historical playback across the last twelve months

A time scrubber over Open-Meteo's free archive. Watch the smog season arrive and lift; watch monsoon rainfall spike.

Why it scoresCheap, visually striking, and it proves seasonality, which is what justifies the forecasting features to a sceptical judge.
Citizen

The contribution layer the brief names as missing

The gap statement is explicit: Lahore has no system that "lets citizens contribute verified data of their own." Verified is the operative word. A report form is table stakes, the verification pipeline is the feature.

C01EFFORT M
RELIMPUX

Geotagged citizen reports

Photo, location, category. Uncollected waste, standing water, open manhole, dead streetlight, illegal dumping. Works on a cheap Android phone in two taps.

Why it scoresMirrors Suthra Punjab's own geo-tagged worker photographs, which means your data model is already shaped to merge with theirs the day access opens.
C02EFFORT M
INNTECFEA

The verification pipeline

Score each report's confidence from corroboration count, geographic plausibility, EXIF timestamp consistency, submitter history, and cross-checks against sensor and satellite data. Surface the confidence on the map instead of treating every report as fact.

Why it scoresThis is the word the brief chose and nobody else will build. It is also the honest answer to the obvious judge question: how do you stop people spamming it?
C03EFFORT S
IMPRWA

Clustering into hotspots and escalation summaries

Nine reports on one street become one hotspot with a severity, not nine pins. Generate a weekly per-UC summary an official could forward without editing.

Why it scoresReduces noise into something a department can act on. Individual complaints get ignored; a ranked hotspot list with counts does not.
C04EFFORT S
IMPRWA

Public accountability view

Open versus resolved reports, median response time, tracked by area and category, visible to everyone.

Why it scoresTransparency creates the pressure that makes the reporting loop close. It is also the feature that gives the platform a reason to exist after the hackathon.
AI

Assistance, not decoration

The brief lists AI assistants as a welcome presentation mode. The trap is a chatbot bolted to the corner answering questions the map already answers. Every item here has to produce something the map cannot.

A01EFFORT M
INNUXTEC

Natural language to map query

"Show me schools within 2km of an area where AQI is above 200" compiles to a structured filter and redraws the map. Always show the generated query so the user can see what was asked.

Why it scoresRemoves the GIS skill requirement, which is the real accessibility barrier for the citizens and councillors this is meant to serve. Showing the compiled query keeps it verifiable rather than magical.
A02EFFORT S
CLRIMPRWA

Daily situation brief per union council

A short written summary generated strictly from the numbers on record. Today's readings, what changed, what is forecast, what needs attention. With every claim carrying its source and timestamp.

Why it scoresThis is what an operations centre actually produces every morning. Grounding it in retrieved values rather than free generation is also your defence against a judge asking whether the AI is making things up.
A03EFFORT S
UXCLR

"Why is this area red?"

Click any shaded area and get a plain-language explanation of which inputs drove the score and by how much.

Why it scoresExplainability, at almost no cost, on a composite index that would otherwise look arbitrary. Directly serves Clarity of the Proposed Solution.
Access

User experience and accessibility

A standalone judging criterion, and the one most teams treat as polish they will get to if there is time. There will not be time. Assign it to someone on day one.

X01EFFORT M
UXIMPRWA

Urdu, with proper right-to-left layout

Full interface translation and RTL mirroring, including the generated briefs and alerts.

Why it scoresA civic tool for Lahore that only speaks English serves the people who least need it. Strongest Real-World Application signal available, and very few teams will do it.
X02EFFORT S
UXTEC

Colourblind-safe severity palettes

Use viridis or cividis ramps rather than default red-to-green. Encode severity with pattern or label as well as hue, so the map survives greyscale printing and the roughly one in twelve men with colour vision deficiency.

Why it scoresNearly free, and it is the accessibility failure most likely to be sitting in the judging panel's own eyes.
X03EFFORT S
UX

A data table behind every map layer

Every visual layer has a sortable table equivalent reachable by keyboard and readable by a screen reader. Full keyboard navigation and visible focus states throughout.

Why it scoresMaps are inherently inaccessible to screen readers. The table is not a consolation prize. It is also faster for the power user comparing twenty areas.
X04EFFORT M
FEARWAUX

Low-bandwidth and offline mode

Install as a PWA, cache map tiles and last known readings, degrade to a text summary when the connection drops.

Why it scoresThe people most exposed to bad air and flooding are the least likely to be on fast, unmetered data. A tool that fails on a weak connection fails exactly where it is needed.
X05EFFORT S
UXRWA

Built mobile-first, tested on a cheap Android

Design for a 360px screen on a mid-range phone, then scale up. Borrow an actual budget handset and run the demo on it.

Why it scoresYour users are on phones. Your judges may check. A desktop-only map is a prototype of the wrong product.
Credibility

Feasibility, scalability and the rules

Two judging criteria and one Code of Conduct obligation live in this band. All of it is cheap.

R01EFFORT S
FEATEC

A documented adapter interface for future official feeds

One typed contract every source implements, live or mocked. Adding Punjab EPA later means writing one adapter, not touching the app.

Why it scores"Scalable so that additional government datasets can easily be integrated over time" is a stated success condition. An interface plus two mock implementations is the proof.
R02EFFORT S
CLRFEA

Provenance on every number

Hover or tap any value to see its source, retrieval time and licence. No unattributed figures anywhere in the interface.

Why it scoresServes Clarity, and discharges the Code of Conduct's requirement to acknowledge third-party datasets, code and AI-generated content as a designed feature rather than a footnote.
R03EFFORT S
TECFEA

Admitted uncertainty

Lahore has few public air sensors. Where you interpolate, say so, and shade confidence on the map. Show sensor positions so coverage gaps are visible rather than hidden by smooth colour.

Why it scoresCounterintuitive but reliable: a technical judge trusts the team that draws its own error bars far more than the team whose interpolated surface implies sensors that do not exist.
R04EFFORT S
FEARWA

Open read API and GeoJSON export

Publish your computed layers, meaning access scores, hotspots and exposure counts, so researchers, journalists and other teams can build on them. Served under ODbL, because layers derived from the OpenStreetMap road graph carry its share-alike clause. Your code stays MIT.

Why it scoresTurns your project from an app into infrastructure, a materially stronger answer to Potential for Real-World Application.
R05EFFORT S
CLR

An attribution and licensing page

Every dataset, library, model and AI-assisted contribution, listed with its licence.

Why it scoresExplicitly required: teams must acknowledge significant third-party resources and must not violate data or content licences. An hour of work that removes a disqualification risk.

The shape this implies

Everything live and keyless runs client-side. Everything keyed runs through a thin server route. Everything gated gets downloaded, trimmed to Lahore and committed as a static asset before judging starts. That split is not a compromise. The server route layer is the demonstration that an official EPA or Suthra Punjab feed can slot in later without a rebuild, which is exactly what the brief asks you to design for.

Demo

The ninety seconds that decide it

Judges remember one moment. Build backwards from this sequence. Anything that does not serve it is a stretch goal, whatever else this document says.

  1. Search a real neighbourhood.Shalimar Town. No login, no setup, no explanation needed.

  2. The profile resolves live.Current AQI, and the number of residents breathing it right now.

  3. Access, by road, not by circle.Nearest hospital 19 minutes. 12,400 people beyond a 15-minute drive from emergency care.

  4. Look forward, not just at now.Smog crossing hazardous in 41 hours. These four union councils, this arrival window.

  5. The system makes a recommendation.A clinic sited here brings 31,000 of those people inside fifteen minutes. Here is the second-best site, and the third.

  6. Hand over the mouse.Let a judge drop their own pin and watch the coverage surface redraw.

  7. Close in Urdu, on a phone.Same brief, right to left, on a budget Android, exported as one page a councillor can print.

  8. Flip on the official feed.Toggle the mocked EPA adapter. The map keeps working. Nothing was rebuilt.

Team

Five people, five lanes

The most common five-person failure is four people on the frontend and nobody on the story. Someone must own the pitch from hour one, Clarity of the Proposed Solution is a judged criterion, not a soft skill.

Geospatial

Boundaries, population grids, tiling, isochrones, the access score. Owns S02, D02, D03.

Backend and adapters

Server routes, key handling, caching, the adapter contract, verification scoring, the public API. Owns S04, C02, R01.

Frontend and map

The map canvas, layer toggles, what-if interaction, comparison view, mobile. Owns S01, D05, X05.

AI and language

Grounded briefs, natural-language query, explanations, Urdu and RTL. Owns A01 to A03, X01.

Design, access and pitch

Information design, WCAG compliance, the printable summary, the demo script, the deck. Owns S03, X02 to X04, and the ninety seconds.

Three ways this goes wrong

Scope. Twelve finished features beat twenty-five broken ones, and a judge can only see what works on the day. Live dependencies. Overpass and Nominatim are shared public infrastructure with rate limits. Cache every response and keep a recorded fallback so a throttled endpoint cannot kill the demo. Untested claims. If you say the smog forecast is accurate, backtest it against the Open-Meteo archive first; a judge who asks and gets a shrug discounts everything else you said.
